# Project Continuity & Cinematic AI Video Prompt Engineering System

## 1. Project Context and Objectives

- **Core Production Paradigm:** 
  - Every project produces video prompts partitioned into exact **5- to 10-second clips** (matching AI model temporal constraints: 5s, 7s, or 10s max generation window).
  - Each clip is authored with complete, deep, multi-dimensional cinematic direction: Camera/Shot design, frame-by-frame micro-action breakdown (with timestamps like 0.0–0.5s, 0.5–1.5s), Lighting, Background/Environment, VFX/CGI layers, Motion Graphics, Color Grade, Layered Sound Design (3 tiers), Voiceover direction, and Music scoring.

  - Every sequence features a dedicated **Scroll-Stopper Hook** (0–3s) engineered using the attention framework (1 primary hook + 2–3 supporting elements, first-frame lock, sound synchronization, curiosity gap).

## 3. Implementation Conventions & Prompt Engineering Standards

When generating prompts for the user, ALWAYS adhere to the **Ultra-Detailed Director's Protocol**:

### A. The Minimum Depth Requirement
- Never output brief, generic, or single-sentence prompts.
- Prompts must be comprehensive director's manuscripts (typically 800–2,000+ words per scene or multi-scene sequence).
- Avoid vague buzzwords like "make it very cinematic". Instead, describe exact physical optics, light vectors, and micro-movements.

### B. Required Sections in Every Master Prompt
Every sequence prompt must contain:
1. **GLOBAL CINEMATIC SPECIFICATIONS (The Continuity Anchor):**
   - Film stock & format (e.g., Kodak 35mm 500T grain, 24fps, anamorphic ratio).
   - Lens optics & depth of field (e.g., anamorphic horizontal flare, f/1.2 shallow focus, oval bokeh).
   - Character visual anchor (facial traits, age, exact clothing, physical gestures, consistency locks).
   - Palette & color grade (shadows, highlights, skin tone treatments).
   - Global sound & music theme.
2. **FRAME-BY-FRAME MICRO-DIRECTION:**
   - Divide the clip into precise timestamps (e.g., Frame 1: 0.0–0.5s, Frame 2: 0.5–1.5s, Frame 3: 1.5–2.5s, etc.).
   - Explicitly detail subject micro-movements (breathing, blinks, knuckle tension, tear formation, pupil dilation).
3. **CINEMATOGRAPHY & CAMERA BEHAVIOR:**
   - Specific camera motion (macro, push-in, orbit, crane-up, rack focus).
   - Explain the narrative purpose of every movement.
4. **LIGHTING DESIGN:**
   - Key light, fill light, rim light, volumetric god rays, practical lights, falloff.
5. **BACKGROUND / ENVIRONMENT:**
   - Foreground, midground, background layers, atmospheric haze, spatial depth.
6. **VFX / CGI LAYER:**
   - Particles (dust, embers, motes), cloth simulation, digital doubles, optical lens flares, particle dissolves.
7. **MOTION GRAPHICS & TYPOGRAPHY:**
   - Letter-spacing, fade rates, font family (serif/sans), subtle film grain breathing.
8. **SOUND DESIGN (3 Tiers):**
   - Tier 1: Close foley/textures (breath, paper crinkle, skin contact, cloth rustle).
   - Tier 2: Mid environmental ambience (waves, wind, footsteps).
   - Tier 3: Deep distant atmosphere (city hum, traffic, low drone).
9. **VOICEOVER DELIVERY:**
   - Exact text, emotional register, pacing, mic proximity, vocal cracks, breath pauses.
10. **MUSIC SCORING:**
    - Exact instrumentation (solo grand piano, sustained viola, string swell), time signature (e.g., 3/4 waltz, 4/4 walking pulse), chords, emotional inflection points.
11. **BUILT-IN TRANSITION:**
    - Explicit mechanism carrying into the next 5–10s clip (white-gold wipe, hard cut on motion, vertical crane lift).

### C. Scroll-Stopper Hook Protocol (First 0–3 Seconds)
- Based on `video-hook-director`:
  - Formula: 1 Primary Attention Trigger + 2–3 Supporting Elements.
  - Zero empty setup: First frame must have action or intense visual contact immediately.
  - Sound sync: Silence before impact → high-definition foley drop/hit → ambient rush.
  - Curiosity gap: A question or mystery must form in the viewer's mind before 1.5s.

## 6. Known Bugs, Limitations, and Unresolved Tasks

- **AI Video Generator Limitations:**
  - Single generation limits: Most state-of-the-art models (Veo, Kling, Runway) generate 5–10 seconds per run. Prompts MUST respect clip duration boundaries.
  - Face consistency across cuts: Generative video models can drift character identity across prompts. The `GLOBAL CINEMATIC SPECIFICATIONS` anchor block is mandatory on every generation to anchor character face, hair, and clothing.
- **Unresolved Tasks:**
  - Generation of companion 9:16 vertical versions for Scenes 2–6 (Scene 1 hook was drafted for 9:16).
  - Creating negative prompt lists tailored per target model (e.g., negative embeddings for Kling vs. Runway).

- **User feedback:** User strongly rejected brief output: *"what is this why you not writeing prompt by adding too much detiled direction... make a prompt is minimum 1000 words longs all cimamatic direction... each freams 5000 words"*.

  - Divided every 5–7s shot into 5–6 discrete micro-frames with timestamps (`FRAME 1 (0.0–0.5s): ...`).
  - Separated every layer into dedicated capitalized headers (`CINEMATOGRAPHY:`, `SHOT DESIGN:`, `LIGHTING BREAKDOWN:`, `BACKGROUND / ENVIRONMENT:`, `VFX / CGI LAYER:`, `SOUND DESIGN:`, `VOICEOVER:`, `MUSIC:`).

- **What happened:** Explanatory text was mixed with prompt text, requiring the user to manually edit.
- **Fix:** Provided clearly delimited markdown blocks marked `COPY-PASTE READY PROMPT` alongside the full director's analysis.

## 8. Testing and Verification Procedures

Before delivering any video prompt to the user, conduct this self-audit:
1. **Duration Check:** Is the prompt explicitly measured for a 5-second, 7-second, or 10-second generation window?
2. **Micro-Frame Check:** Are there at least 4–6 timestamped frame micro-actions detailing subtle physical actions (pupil motion, breathing, cloth movement)?
3. **Multi-Sensory Audio Check:** Does the sound design have all 3 tiers (foley, ambience, distant low drone) plus voiceover direction and exact musical key/tempo?
4. **Visual Hierarchy Check:** Are camera movement, lens optics, lighting vectors, and VFX explicitly defined with zero generic filler words?
5. **Continuity Check:** Does the scene start where the previous scene ended and finish with a defined transition into the next scene?
6. **Volume/Word Count Check:** Is the prompt deep, authoritative, and exhaustive (minimum 800–1,500 words per scene)?
